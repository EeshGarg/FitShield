Before you turn on app blocking

To show a mindful pause when you open a food app you chose to block, FitShield uses an Android accessibility service. Here is exactly what that means.

  • What it reads: only the package name of the app currently in the foreground — for example com.dd.doordash. Nothing else.
  • What it never reads: your screen contents, text you type, messages, passwords, or anything inside any app. The service is configured with canRetrieveWindowContent="false", so the system does not hand it that data at all.
  • What it is used for: deciding whether to show the FitShield pause screen. That is the only use.
  • Where it goes: nowhere. It is compared against the on-device list and discarded. No accounts, no cloud, no analytics, nothing uploaded, nothing logged.
  • Turning it off: switch FitShield off in Android's Accessibility settings at any time.

You do not have to enable this. Without it, FitShield cannot pause food apps on this phone.

        Privacy & about

        Local-first. No accounts, no cloud, no sync, no telemetry, no analytics. FitShield does not upload your browsing history, and never logs the apps you open.

        The Android app blocks food-delivery apps only: an optional app blocker reads just which app is in the foreground (never screen content) to show a mindful pause. Websites are blocked by the FitShield browser extension, not by this app. It does not tunnel or inspect any network traffic.

        • This Android build is preview quality.
        • The schedule and the timer above do apply — the schedule limits when app blocking applies, the timer is the countdown on the pause screen a blocked app shows.
        How to test this preview
        1. Turn FitShield on, then enable the FitShield accessibility service under "Block apps on this phone".
        2. Open a blocked food app — the FitShield pause screen should appear. Open any other app — it should open normally.
        3. Turn FitShield off to stop blocking. Uninstalling also stops it.

        FitShield is free and local-first. If it has saved you an order or two, a coffee is always appreciated — never required.
