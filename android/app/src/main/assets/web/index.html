Welcome to FitShield

A local-first nudge against impulse food-delivery and fast-food ordering. No accounts, no cloud, no tracking — everything stays on your device.

  • Turn on FitShield to block delivery & fast-food sites; optionally block their apps too.
  • See what actually happened: every ordering page FitShield interrupted, counted on this device and nowhere else.
  • Make it yours: theme colors, language and more below.

Before you turn on app blocking

To show a mindful pause when you open a food app you chose to block, FitShield uses an Android accessibility service. Here is exactly what that means.

  • What it reads: only the package name of the app currently in the foreground — for example com.dd.doordash. Nothing else.
  • What it never reads: your screen contents, text you type, messages, passwords, or anything inside any app. The service is configured with canRetrieveWindowContent="false", so the system does not hand it that data at all.
  • What it is used for: deciding whether to show the FitShield pause screen. That is the only use.
  • Where it goes: nowhere. It is compared against the on-device list and discarded. No accounts, no cloud, no analytics, nothing uploaded, nothing logged.
  • Turning it off: switch FitShield off in Android's Accessibility settings at any time. Site blocking keeps working without it.

You do not have to enable this. FitShield blocks delivery and fast-food websites without it — app blocking is the optional extra.

                      Privacy & about

                      Local-first. No accounts, no cloud, no sync, no telemetry, no analytics. FitShield does not upload your browsing history, and never logs the sites or apps you open.

                      On Android, FitShield blocks in two independent ways: a local VPN that resets connections to blocked sites by the name in the connection (TLS SNI) — no traffic tunnelling to any server, no HTTPS inspection, no certificates, no content inspection, and DNS is never intercepted or changed; and an optional app blocker that reads only which app is in the foreground (never screen content) to show a mindful pause.

                      FitShield asks Android to let it start at device startup (RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED), and uses it for exactly one thing: if site blocking was on when your phone shut down — or when FitShield updated itself — it is turned back on when the phone comes back. If you had it off, nothing starts and nothing appears. If Android needs your VPN confirmation again, FitShield shows one notification telling you protection is off, with a single tap to restore it, rather than starting anything behind your back or saying nothing. Nothing else runs at startup and nothing is uploaded.

                      • This Android build is preview quality. Site blocking works alongside encrypted / Private DNS. There is no custom block page for HTTPS sites (that would require interception FitShield won't do) — blocked sites simply fail to connect.
                      • The schedule and the timer above do apply — the schedule to both site and app blocking, the timer to the pause screen a blocked app shows.
                      • Country and category selections are saved on device but do not change what the connection filter blocks. It already blocks every curated delivery and fast-food brand, and those selections only ever add to that set, so there is nothing left for them to add.
                      How to test this preview
                      1. Enable and approve the one-time VPN consent dialog.
                      2. Open doordash.com — it should fail to load; open wikipedia.org — it should load normally.
                      3. For app blocking, turn it on above and enable the FitShield accessibility service, then open a blocked app.
                      4. Restart the phone while FitShield is on — site blocking should be on again once it comes back, without you doing anything. If Android wants your VPN confirmation again you get one notification instead, and tapping it restores blocking.
                      5. Turn FitShield off, then restart the phone — it must still be off, with no notification.
                      6. Disable to stop filtering. Uninstalling also stops it.

                      FitShield is free and local-first. If it has saved you an order or two, a coffee is always appreciated — never required.
