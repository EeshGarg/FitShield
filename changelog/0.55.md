# 0.55 — The block succeeded. Now what?

*2026-08-09*

FitShield has always been good at interrupting an ordering page. It was much
weaker at the moment straight after: two fixed recipe cards, no way to ask for
something else, and no idea what was in your kitchen. This release is about that
moment.

## The block page is a decision, not a dashboard

One column, one question at a time:

- **why** the page was interrupted (domain, rule type, category, markets),
- **how much** of the pause is left,
- **one** thing you could make instead — with **Show another**, and
  **Closest / Fastest / No cooking / Microwave** filters,
- **three ways out**, all visible: go back, choose the alternative, or continue
  on purpose.

An optional, skippable question — "What brought you here?" — shapes what you are
shown for that visit only. The answer is never stored. Saying you need to place a
real order, or that you are ordering for someone else, takes you straight to the
pass options instead of making you sit out a countdown for something FitShield
was never meant to prevent.

## Continuing is now scoped

Instead of switching the whole extension off, you can let **this site** through
for your own **site open time** (5 minutes until you change it), **10 minutes**,
**30 minutes**, or **until you close this tab** — or turn off **all blocking**
for **30 minutes** or **until tomorrow**.

The first entry is labelled from your own setting rather than a hard-coded
number, so it can never quote a duration the pass does not honour, and it is
hidden when it would simply repeat the fixed 10- or 30-minute entry.

Every option is a scope plus a duration, and says so. Each button carries its
scope in words — *This site only* or *All blocking*. There is deliberately no
"just this once": `declarativeNetRequest` rules are global and report nothing
back when they match, so the extension cannot see a single visit happen and
stand down after it. Doing that honestly would need a browser-wide navigation
listener, which is a permission and an observation surface FitShield will not
take for one menu entry.

Every pass carries an absolute expiry timestamp and a ceiling on the duration it
was granted for, so passes end correctly across a browser restart, a suspended
service worker, system sleep, a clock change, and an extension update.

## 88 alternatives you can actually cook

The catalog was rebuilt from 24 sketches into 88 entries: **46 full recipes** and
**42 quick fixes**. Every one has exact servings and quantities, temperatures and
durations where heat is involved, a physical doneness cue you can check, hands-on
effort recorded separately from elapsed time, equipment, the major allergens,
realistic substitutions, and storage guidance.

Every craving in the taxonomy has a vegetarian answer, and every craving that can
physically be made without heat has one that needs only a microwave.

Coverage spans pizza, burgers, fried chicken, chicken sandwiches, wings, fries,
tacos, burritos, rice bowls, noodles, pasta, sandwiches, breakfast, coffee-shop
drinks, desserts, ice cream, bakery cravings, late-night and convenience food,
and Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Indian, Middle Eastern, Mediterranean and
Latin American cooking. 68 entries are vegetarian or stricter and 20 of those are
vegan; 26 involve no heat at all, and 37 need nothing hotter than a microwave.

Calories, where they appear at all, are an honest range and never the organising
principle.

## Suggestions are matched, not guessed

A deterministic local score, with no AI and no network, weighs the blocked
brand's own category and specialties, your diet and allergens (hard filters — you
will never be shown something that breaks them), your pantry and equipment, how
much time you have, your favourites, and what you were shown recently. Asking for
another walks the ranking rather than shuffling it, so you can step back to a
suggestion you liked.

## Honest statistics

Counters now name only what FitShield can observe:

| Now | Instead of |
| --- | --- |
| Ordering pages interrupted | "Blocked visits" treated as prevented orders |
| Times you left · Times you continued | (not recorded at all) |
| Temporary passes used | (not recorded at all) |
| Alternatives shown to you · Alternatives you chose · Alternatives you marked as made | "Calories avoided", added the moment a card was displayed |

Choosing an alternative is an **intention**. Confirming you made it is a
**separate, optional** step you take yourself from the popup, and declining it
records nothing.

Settings' **Your Stats** shows those seven counters and nothing else. One
estimate remains, switched **off** by default: the meals you confirmed you made,
times a price you set. It prints that arithmetic under the figure, so it can
never be mistaken for a measurement. It is deliberately *not* based on how many
pages were interrupted — an interruption tells FitShield nothing about whether
an order would have happened.

"Calories avoided" is gone. It counted a recipe card being displayed, and
FitShield cannot see what anyone ate. The number you had is preserved in your
data rather than shown.

Your existing numbers were carried over, not reset.

## Also new

- **Friction presets** — light, standard, strict. Each writes into the same
  editable values, so a preset is a starting point, not a mode. None is
  irreversible.
- **Schedules** — multiple windows, different hours per weekday, and overnight
  windows that belong to the day they start on. Presets first, the full editor
  behind a disclosure. "Block until tomorrow" expires on its own.
- **Your kitchen** — diet, allergens, equipment, and the staples you usually keep
  in. Diet and allergens filter; pantry and equipment only reorder. No
  quantities, no dates, no shopping history.
- **Your own alternatives** — add the things you actually make. They compete in
  the ranking, are included in your backup, and survive upgrades.
- **A weekly summary** — local, optional, dismissible, counts only. No scores, no
  streaks, no rankings, no projections.
- **Preview mode** — see exactly what an interruption looks like without visiting
  an ordering site. Nothing is recorded and nothing is unblocked.
- **Repeat-visit friction** — one small, capped, explained addition to the pause
  if you return to a site soon after continuing. Never an escalating lockout, and
  it can be switched off.
- **Reporting** — compose a report, see verbatim what it would say, then copy it
  to your clipboard. Paths and query strings are stripped to a bare domain.
  Nothing leaves your device unless you paste it somewhere yourself. There is no
  "send" button: an address FitShield cannot prove will receive mail is a dead
  end dressed up as a feature.

## Categories now name what the brand actually sells

The block page and the statistics used to be handed one of the blocking engine's
internal **rule buckets** as a brand's category. Every fast-food interruption was
therefore filed under *Fastfood* — a word no dataset carries and Settings' own
category picker does not offer — and every custom site under *Custom*.

Both surfaces now read the brand's real curated category from the blocklist:
*Pizza*, *Burger*, *Fast Casual*, *Coffee*, *Chicken*, *Tea*, *Bakery*,
*Dessert*, *Grocery*, *Mexican*, *Sandwich*, *Seafood*, *Smoothie*,
*Korean Food*, *Asian*, *Restaurant*, *Catering*, *Courier*, *Meal Kit*,
*Meal Delivery*, *Restaurant Discovery* and *Delivery* — the 22 categories the
current data uses, each with a translated display name.

The block page's **Category** row also stops repeating itself. When the category
would only restate the rule that fired it — "Rule: Fast food / Category: Fast
food" — the row is hidden instead of printed twice, and a site you added yourself
shows no category row at all rather than the word *Custom*.

### What you will see change after updating

**Your "most blocked categories" list will lose its *Fastfood* and *Custom*
rows.** They were mislabels, and a one-time repair removes them the first time
the updated extension refreshes its rules.

Read precisely, because the distinction matters:

- It removes only those two keys, only from the category breakdown, and only
  when they are present. It runs once, is safe to run again, and touches nothing
  else.
- **No interruption is forgotten.** The same events were also counted in
  *Ordering pages interrupted*, in most-blocked **sites**, and in most-blocked
  **countries** — none of which was ever mislabeled. Those totals do not move.
- The repair does **not** re-file the old counts under the right categories,
  because the category they belonged to was never recorded. So the category list
  may look sparse straight after the update and will fill in again as you use it.
- *Delivery* is **not** removed. It is a genuine curated category carried by
  hundreds of delivery brands, not a rule bucket.

## One schedule control instead of two

Settings used to offer a simple start/end time pair *and* the full window editor,
both writing the same setting. Whichever you reached second silently overwrote
the first, and the simple pair could not express what the editor could. The pair
is gone; the schedule is asked once, in the section that owns it — presets first,
the multi-window editor behind a disclosure.

The popup keeps its own start/end pair as an at-a-glance surface. It disables
itself whenever your schedule has more shape than two time inputs can hold, so it
can never quietly flatten a multi-window or per-weekday schedule.

## A currency picker that says what it does

The first entry of the currency list read as a globe emoji followed by a currency
name, which looked like just another currency rather than the automatic option.
It now reads **"Match my language — <currency> (<symbol>)"**, so the entry states
both what it does and what it will pick.

## Compatibility

Existing profiles migrate automatically. Settings, custom blocklists, whitelists,
schedules, statistics, and backups are preserved; the old counters remain in
place alongside the new ones. Backups written by earlier versions still import.

The one deliberate exception is the category-breakdown repair described above,
which drops two mislabeled keys and no counts of events.

Chrome and Firefox are both supported as before, and the Android app shares the
same catalog and rules.
